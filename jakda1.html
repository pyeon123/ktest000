Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. How do Koreans say "Small"?

작다([jak-da/small]) is the basic adjective for small size. In polite speech, say 작아요([ja-ga-yo]). The opposite is 크다([keu-da]), "big". Example: 이 방이 작아요 (This room is small).

Q2. How do I use "small" before a noun?

Korean adjectives change their form before a noun. 작다 becomes 작은 (ja-geun), so say 작은 가방 (a small bag), 작은 방 (a small room), or 작은 건물 (a small building). Without a noun, use 작아요: 가방이 작아요 (The bag is small).

Q3. Does 작다 have other grammar forms?

Yes. 작았어요([ja-ga-sseo-yo/was small]) is past tense, 너무 작아요([neo-mu ja-ga-yo/too small]) means too small, 작지 않아요([jak-ji a-na-yo/isn't small]) is negative. For shopping, say 더 작은 거 주세요([deo ja-geun geo ju-se-yo/a smaller one, please]). 작다 is a regular adjective, so ㅏ/ㅓ conjugation is simple: 작 + 아요 → 작아요.

EPS TOPIK Learn More: How to Say "Small" in Korean - 작다([jak-da/small])

What does "작다([jak-da/small])" mean?

작다([jak-da/small]) means "small". It is the basic adjective for small size, such as a small room, a small bag, or a small building. The opposite is 크다([keu-da]) "big".

Casual: 작아 (ja-ga)
Polite: 작아요 (ja-ga-yo)
Formal: 작습니다 (jak-seum-ni-da)
Before a noun: 작은 (ja-geun) - 작은 가방 (a small bag)

Grammar Breakdown

작다([jak-da/small]) = 작 (stem: small) + 다 (dictionary ending)
Regular adjective: 작 + 아요 → 작아요 (no irregular change)
작았어요([ja-ga-sseo-yo/was small]) = 작 + 았어요 → 작았어요 (small + past)
너무 작아요([neo-mu ja-ga-yo/too small]) = 너무 (too) + 작아요 (is small)
작지 않아요([jak-ji a-na-yo/isn't small]) = 작 + 지 않아요 (small + isn't)
작은 거([ja-geun geo/a small one]) = 작 + ㄴ (before a noun) + 거 (thing)
더 작은 거 주세요([deo ja-geun geo ju-se-yo/a smaller one, please]) = 더 (more) + 작은 거 (small one) + 주세요 (please give)

When to Use It (EPS-TOPIK Style)

1. Size: “이 방이 작아요? (i bang-i ja-ga-yo?)”
2. Past: 작았어요([ja-ga-sseo-yo/was small]) was small.
3. Too much: 너무 작아요([neo-mu ja-ga-yo/too small]) too small.
4. Negative: 작지 않아요([jak-ji a-na-yo/isn't small]) isn't small.
5. Shopping: 더 작은 거 주세요([deo ja-geun geo ju-se-yo/a smaller one, please]) a smaller one, please.

Real-Life Examples - A-B-A-B Dialogue (EPS-TOPIK)

A: 이 방이 작아요? A: i bang-i ja-ga-yo?

A: Is this room small?

B: 네, 좀 작아요. B: ne, jom ja-ga-yo.

B: Yes, it's a bit small.

A: 몇 명이 자요? A: myeot myeong-i ja-yo?

A: How many people sleep here?

B: 네 명이 자요. B: ne myeong-i ja-yo.

B: Four people sleep here. - 작다([jak-da/small])

A: 이 신발 어때요? A: i sin-bal eo-ttae-yo?

A: How are these shoes?

B: 너무 작아요. B: neo-mu ja-ga-yo.

B: They are too small.

A: 큰 거 드릴까요? A: keun geo deu-ril-kka-yo?

A: Shall I give you a bigger one?

B: 네, 주세요. B: ne, ju-se-yo.

B: Yes, please. - 너무 작아요([neo-mu ja-ga-yo/too small])

A: 이 옷은 커요. A: i o-seun keo-yo.

A: This shirt is big.

B: 더 작은 거 드릴까요? B: deo ja-geun geo deu-ril-kka-yo?

B: Shall I give you a smaller one?

A: 네, 더 작은 거 주세요. A: ne, deo ja-geun geo ju-se-yo.

A: Yes, a smaller one, please.

B: 잠깐만 기다리세요. B: jam-kkan-man gi-da-ri-se-yo.

B: Please wait a moment. - 더 작은 거 주세요([deo ja-geun geo ju-se-yo/a smaller one, please])
