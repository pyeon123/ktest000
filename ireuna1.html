Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. When do Koreans say "Stand up" — 일어나세요 (ireonaseyo)?

Koreans use it in classrooms, meetings, or events to ask people to stand politely.

Q2. Is "Stand up" — 일어나세요 (ireonaseyo) polite enough?

Yes. "일어나세요 (ireonaseyo)" is polite and commonly used by teachers or leaders. You can also say "일어나 (ireona)" casually with friends.

Q3. What should I say in a classroom?

You can say "Everyone stand up please" — 모두 일어나세요 (modu ireonaseyo).

Learn More: How to Say "Stand Up" in Korean

What does "일어나세요 (ireonaseyo)" mean?

일어나세요 means "Please stand up / Get up." 일어나다 (ireonada) = to get up, stand up, wake up, -세요 (-seyo) = polite imperative. Common in classrooms, meetings, events, ceremonies. Shows respect for hierarchy.

Polite: 일어나세요 (i-reo-na-se-yo) - Standard polite
Formal: 일어나십시오 (i-reo-na-sip-si-o) - Official, formal
Casual: 일어나 (i-reo-na) - Friends, family

Grammar Breakdown

일어나다 (ireonada) = to get up, stand up, wake up
-세요 (-seyo) = polite imperative ending
-십시오 (-sipsio) = formal imperative
→ 일어나세요 = Please stand up.

When to Use It

1. Classroom: Teacher enters, national anthem.
2. Meetings/events: Speaker asks audience to stand.
3. Weddings/ceremonies: Greeting guests.

Real-Life Examples

모두 일어나세요. 선생님이 오셨어요. mo-du i-reo-na-se-yo. seon-saeng-nim-i o-syeoss-eo-yo.

Everyone stand up. Teacher is here.

모두 일어나십시오. 국민의례를 하겠습니다. mo-du i-reo-na-sip-si-o. guk-min-ui-rye-reul ha-get-seum-ni-da.

Everyone please stand for the national ceremony. (Formal)

야, 일어나! 늦었어! ya, i-reo-na! neu-jeoss-eo!

Hey, get up! You're late! (Casual)

자리에서 일어나세요. ja-ri-e-seo i-reo-na-se-yo.

Please stand up from your seat.

Similar Expressions

• 일어나 (ireona) - Get up (casual)
• 기상! (gisang!) - Wake up! (military)
