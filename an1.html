Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. When do Koreans say "Sit down" — 앉으세요 (aneuseyo)?

Koreans use it to politely invite someone to sit, often as a host, teacher, or in restaurants.

Q2. Is "Sit down" — 앉으세요 (aneuseyo) polite enough for guests?

Yes. "앉으세요 (aneuseyo)" is polite and commonly used when welcoming guests. You can also say "앉아 (anja)" for a more casual tone with friends.

Q3. What should I say to offer a seat?

You can say "Please sit here" — 여기 앉으세요 (yeogi aneuseyo) or "Sit comfortably" — 편하게 앉으세요 (pyeonhage aneuseyo).

Learn More: How to Say "Sit Down" in Korean

What does "앉으세요 (aneuseyo)" mean?

앉으세요 means "Please sit down." 앉다 (anda) = to sit, -으세요 (-euseyo) = polite imperative. Used to politely invite guests, customers, students to take a seat. Shows hospitality and respect in Korean culture.

Polite: 앉으세요 (an-eu-se-yo) - Standard polite invitation
Formal: 앉으십시오 (an-eu-sip-si-o) - Business, formal events
Casual: 앉아 (an-ja) - Friends, family

Grammar Breakdown

앉다 (anda) = to sit
-으세요 (-euseyo) = polite imperative
-으십시오 (-eusipsio) = formal imperative
→ 앉으세요 = Please sit down.

When to Use It

1. Welcoming guests: Home, office, restaurant.
2. Classroom/meeting: Teacher invites students.
3. Hospitality: Makes people feel welcome.

Real-Life Examples

여기 앉으세요. yeo-gi an-eu-se-yo.

Please sit here.

편하게 앉으세요. pyeon-ha-ge an-eu-se-yo.

Please sit comfortably / Make yourself comfortable.

편안하게 앉으십시오. pyeon-an-ha-ge an-eu-sip-si-o.

Please sit comfortably. (Formal)

여기 앉아, 편하게. yeo-gi an-ja, pyeon-ha-ge.

Sit here, comfortably. (Casual)
