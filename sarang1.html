Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. When do Koreans say 사랑해요 (saranghaeyo)?

It's the most common polite way to say "I love you" in daily life - to partners, family, and close friends. Used in dating, anniversaries, and affectionate moments.

Q2. What's the difference between 사랑해요 and 사랑해?

사랑해요 is polite and safe for most situations. 사랑해 is casual, used between very close partners or friends. Both mean "I love you".

Q3. When should I use 사랑합니다?

사랑합니다 is formal and very serious. Great for proposals, wedding vows, or deeply sincere confessions. It shows respect and weight.

Learn More: How to Say "I Love You" in Korean

What does "사랑해요 (saranghaeyo)" mean?

사랑해요 means "I love you" in a polite, warm way. 사랑 (sarang) = love, 하다 → 해요 (hada → haeyo) = to do → polite form. It's the most common way to express love in daily life in Korea - dating, family, and K-drama style romance.

Polite: 사랑해요 (sa-rang-hae-yo) - Standard daily use
Formal: 사랑합니다 (sa-rang-ham-ni-da) - Proposal, vows, serious
Casual: 사랑해 (sa-rang-hae) - Very close partner/friends

Grammar Breakdown

사랑 (sarang) = love (noun)
하다 (hada) = to do (verb)
사랑하다 → 사랑해요 (saranghada → saranghaeyo) = to love → polite
→ 사랑해요 = I love you.

When to Use It

1. Dating / Partner: Daily "I love you"
2. Family: To parents, kids
3. Texting / K-drama: Most romantic scenes use this form

Real-Life Examples

사랑해요. 오늘도 예뻐요. sa-rang-hae-yo. o-neul-do ye-ppeo-yo.

I love you. You're pretty today too.

정말 사랑해요. 영원히 함께해요. jeong-mal sa-rang-hae-yo. yeong-won-hi ham-kke-hae-yo.

I really love you. Let's be together forever.

사랑해. 보고 싶었어. sa-rang-hae. bo-go sip-eoss-eo.

Love you. I missed you. (Casual)

사랑합니다. 결혼해 주세요. sa-rang-ham-ni-da. gyeol-hon-hae ju-se-yo.

I love you. Please marry me. (Formal/Proposal)

Similar Expressions

• 보고 싶어요 (bogo sipeoyo) - I miss you
• 내꺼야 (naekkeoya) - You're mine (cute possessive)
